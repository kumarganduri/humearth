## Problem Statement
The live site is a clay toy globe, a 3-question quiz and a camera dive into a personal diorama. The founder's verdict: it isn't intuitive, it isn't lively, people don't understand what is happening, it needs real numbers and good visualization, and it is too kid-oriented.

The job of v2 is to show how much the world is being affected by AI's growing electricity, carbon and water use. It should be honest enough for experts, clear enough for anyone, and good-looking enough to share.

## What Makes This Cool
- **The headline layer:** in 10 seconds, anyone sees that data centres already use as much electricity as a country, and that AI is the fastest-growing part of it.
- **The hero:** one year slider (2017 to 2035) drives an animated ranking and a fan chart at once, and in stage 2 a night Earth as well. In the ranking, "Data centres" overtakes real countries. The future is a widening fan of possibilities, not a single confident line.
- **Built-in honesty:** every impact carries a MEASURED or EXPLAINED badge, and every number has a range or is labelled as a single published estimate.

## Decisions (this session)
- **Audience:** everyone, in two layers. A headline layer anyone gets in 10 seconds, with a "show me the data" layer underneath. Serious data journalism that a teenager can read. *Why:* the kid framing made real numbers feel like a toy.
- **Headline:** "Data centres already use as much power as a country, and AI is the fastest-growing part." AI is shown as a slice inside the total.
  - **AI share is drawn for today only:** the US-based 26.5 to 38% range, labelled "US share, used as a world estimate".
  - **The "fastest-growing" claim cites the IEA directly** ("AI-focused data centres ... poised to triple" by 2030). No AI-share curve is drawn over the years until a world series is sourced. *Why:* the earlier "AI is as big as a country" mixed up AI with all data centres. All three independent reviewers flagged it as the first thing an expert would attack.
- **Hero layout:** a split screen with the night Earth on one side and the country race on the other, driven by one year slider that plays itself on load and then hands over control. On phones the two stack vertically.
- **What the slider shows** (these rules also settle the earlier open questions):
  - **Countries stay at their latest measured year.** Data centres are compared against countries' use today, as the IEA does. The chart says so: "countries at {table year} levels" (the latest year the chosen country table publishes). When the data centres are at 2025, that one-year gap is labelled too.
  - **Crossing a country, "passes X":**
    - **Earliest year:** where the fan's high edge first exceeds the country's figure.
    - **Latest year:** where the low edge does.
    - If the low edge never crosses by 2035, it says "maybe not by 2035".
    - If data centres already exceed the country in the latest measured year, it says "already passed (by {year})".
    - Between published points, values are filled in at a constant growth rate and drawn dashed.
  - **Water:** shown for today only. Past 2025 the water panel stays at today's figure with "no projection published", greyed out.
  - **Electricity and CO2 over time,** with ranges. CO2 follows grid-intensity assumptions and gets a visibly wider band.
  - **Only world totals move with the slider.** The hub lights show today's middle figure and do not grow by year, because no per-hub history exists.
- **Your part:** a calculator section after the hero.
  - **Framing:** "Your typing is small. Your video isn't. And your voice is the big one." Here "voice" means civic influence: your vote, your city, your employer. It is not AI voice features, so no energy figure is needed for it.
  - **No multiplier:** there is no "if 100 million people used AI like you". It is replaced with "Where does the electricity actually go?": the AI share against the rest of data-centre load, with consumer chat as one visible slice.
- **Removed:** the clay world, the dive, the quiz, the balloon and glass comparisons, and the hiding birds.
- **Not a building-by-building map:** AIxEnergy already maps about 2,560 projects. Hum shows about 27 big places plus country totals, and links out for more depth.
- **Sourcing rule unchanged:** low/middle/high for every figure, quotes checked on the source page, How We Know and the change log kept up to date.
  - **Point estimates:** when a source gives only one number (for example IEA 2024 = 415 TWh), it is stored with low = middle = high. The page labels it "single published estimate", and How We Know explains why.
  - **Derived values** (for example history years calculated from a growth rate) are drawn in a different style (dashed line, hatched bar), not only footnoted.

## Premises (agreed)
1. The future is shown as an IEA range (700 to 1,700 TWh by 2035), never as one line.
2. Hum shows all data centres with AI's share inside, because only the total is well sourced worldwide.
3. Depth lives in the data layer. The globe stays readable.
4. The site looks like serious, beautiful data journalism.
5. Every number follows the sourcing rule.
6. "Today" moves from 2024 to 2025, using the IEA's +17% for 2025.

## Cross-Model Perspective
The Codex opinion failed because Codex is not logged in (error 401). Three independent Claude reviewers ran instead, each with a different brief and no view of the conversation. Full notes: `~/.gstack/projects/ai-footprint/second-opinions-20261003.md`.
- **All three** challenged the headline, which changed as a result.
- **Two** rejected the 100-million multiplier.
- **Data flags:**
  - **Video energy:** the middle and high figures are both 944 Wh.
  - **Grid carbon:** the middle and high figures are both 473 g/kWh, and the high end is too low for coal-heavy hubs.
  - **Hub high ends:** these likely include planned capacity.
- **Ideas adopted into the stages below:**
  - MEASURED/EXPLAINED badges
  - a fan chart with country lines, where crossing points become date ranges
  - a ranked chart of what actually reduces use, including the tiny bars
  - calling out greenwashing
  - live grid carbon in the hub regions
  - Hum Daily, a daily sourced question with a share card
- **Ideas held for later:**
  - "Follow one question", a path from your phone to the server and power plant
  - a literal hum whose sound follows the data
  - data centres drawn as a country-shaped landmass that changes outline by year
  - a forecast scorecard comparing predictions with what happened

## Approaches Considered
- **A, the honest core in 2D only:** chosen as stage 1a/1b, not as the end state.
- **C, Hum Daily as the homepage:** rejected because first-time visitors would get a quiz before the big picture, which is today's problem again.

## Recommended Approach: B, the full vision built in stages, starting with A

### Stage 1a: the honest core (ships first)
**Layout until stage 2:**
- the full-width headline
- the country race and the fan chart side by side, both driven by the year slider (stacked on phones)
- then the calculator

The **Earth posters** stay as a static image until stage 2. Stage 2 replaces the left half of the hero with the 3D night Earth.
1. **Data first:**
   - Add IEA series to `constants.source.json` with checked quotes: 2024 = 415 TWh, 2030 = ~945 TWh (base case), 2035 = ~1,200 TWh (base case), and the 2035 range of 700 to 1,700 TWh.
   - Add 2025 (+17%) and the history back to 2017 (~12% a year).
   - Add data-centre CO2: 180 Mt today, 300 Mt in 2035 (base case), up to 500 Mt (Lift-Off case). Source the 2035 low end from the IEA High-Efficiency case. If no low end is published, it is drawn as "no published low"; it is never filled in.
   - Add the 2030 range from the IEA sensitivity cases.
   - Add a sourced table of country electricity use (Ember or IEA) for the race.
   - **Re-source the video energy and grid-carbon highs.** Grid carbon gets a global range for the calculator, plus per-region figures for the hub regions in stage 3.
   - **Schema:** time series and the country table each get their own validated file in `data/sources/`, with the same rules as constants (https sources, a `checked` field, order checks per year).
   - Label each derived value (for example history years calculated from the growth rate) as "derived" on How We Know.
2. **Headline section:** the honest headline plus "AI inside" nested bars.
3. **Year slider with the country race in 2D,** plus the fan chart with country lines.
4. **Calculator and "Where does the electricity go?"**
5. **Impact grid** (stage 1a covers the measured tiles only; the rest is stage 1b) with MEASURED/EXPLAINED badges:
   - **Measured:** electricity, CO2, water (on-site and at the power plant).
   - **Explained, with named cases:** grid strain and prices, local turbine air, chips and e-waste, land, noise.
6. **"What actually reduces it"** (stage 1b) comes in two parts, because they don't share a unit:
   - **Personal:** ranked bars in Wh per week, computed from the visitor's own calculator inputs (default profile: 20 text answers a day, 3 pictures a week, 1 video a week), with the tiny bars included.
   - **Civic:** a short list without a scale (where data centres are sited, grid disclosure, local permits).
   - **Greenwashing callouts** name practices, not companies, and each one cites a source.
7. **Housekeeping (stage 1a):**
   - Rewrite How We Know.
   - Remove `derive:k`, `mapping.json`, the health scale and the guardrail tests. Update CLAUDE.md's "Guardrail rule" section and the CI steps that run `derive:k`. Replace them with checks on the time series: years in order, low ≤ mid ≤ high, published points match their quotes.
   - Update the E2E tests and copy lint; the kid glossary is replaced by a plain-language list.
   - Retire the clay scene code and the quiz state machine.

### Stage 1b: the rest of the story
- the impact grid's EXPLAINED tiles with named cases
- "What actually reduces it", including the greenwashing callouts

### Stage 2: the 3D night Earth split hero
The night Earth sits next to the race, driven by the same slider, with the hub glows at today's middle figure.
- **Places:** the existing 27 hubs in `data/sources/hubs.source.json`.
- **Glow size:** the middle figure in MW, never the high end. three.js stays a lazy chunk, and the posters remain the fallback when 3D isn't available.

### Stage 3: live grid carbon
Hourly gCO2/kWh for 2 or 3 hub regions, for example "an AI answer in Virginia right now emits about X g". This needs the choice of API and its terms (Electricity Maps, EIA or EirGrid) and possibly a key.

### Stage 4: Hum Daily
A once-a-day sourced higher-or-lower question with a share card and a streak kept on the visitor's device. It can draw on the hubs, countries and per-use figures.

## Success Criteria
- **10-second test:** a first-time adult can say, unprompted, that data centres are as big as a country, that the use is growing fast, and that AI is the fast part.
- **Expert test:** nothing a domain expert would flag as unsourced or conflated. Every number on the page links to How We Know.
- **Phones:** works down to a 320px phone. First-screen JS stays at or under 90 KB gzipped (the existing `check:budget`) with no three.js. Lighthouse checks pass: LCP at most 2.5 s, CLS at most 0.1.
- **Motion and access:**
  - The slider's autoplay doesn't run under `prefers-reduced-motion`: it jumps to today.
  - The slider is a native range input with arrow-key steps and an `aria-valuetext` such as "2030: about 945 terawatt-hours".
  - Every chart has a text equivalent.
- **Return visits** (stages 3 and 4): live grid carbon and Hum Daily give a reason to come back.
