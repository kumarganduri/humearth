# Hum: simulated phone test, 14 November 2026

Twenty AI agents each played a different first-time visitor and followed `user-test-script.md` on the live site. They saw only phone-size screenshots of humearth.org and could tap, scroll and type. **These are simulated people, not real ones.** They are reliable on confusing wording, dead ends, broken screens and missing answers; treat "would share" and "would come back" as weaker signals.

## Score against the script’s pass marks

| Pass mark | Result |
|---|---|
| 7 of 10 can say what it’s for after 10 seconds | 20 of 20 |
| Most play a card and open an answer | 20 of 20 did both |
| At least 3 name something they’d send | 20 of 20 (mostly the bottle-of-water answer) |
| Water task done in under a minute | 20 of 20, all by search, in 2 to 3 actions |
| Would come back tomorrow for Today (not a pass mark) | 0 of 20 |

## What they agreed on

| Finding | Testers (of 20) |
|---|---|
| Wh / TWh figures were hard to follow | 20 |
| Wanted an answer about their own country, state or town | 18 |
| The “not comparable” dot chart was unclear | 17 |
| The “NOBODY KNOWS” stamp on the bottle claim reads wrong: the text shows the claim is too high | 15 |
| Gallons, miles and US examples didn’t fit them | 15 |
| Wanted to know who makes and pays for Hum | 14 |
| Stray “.” in the no-result search box | 18 |
| Headline font and menu jump between loads | 16 |
| Search matched on one common word and showed unrelated answers | 12 |
| The Earth page opens on an empty dark circle | 5 |
