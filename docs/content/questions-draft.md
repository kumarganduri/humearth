# Hum answers your question: draft (2026-10-18)

The 15 questions people actually bring to a site like Hum, each answered with what `data/sources` already holds. This is a draft for judging whether the content is enough; nothing here is on the site yet.

How each answer works: **verdict** in one line, then **the numbers** (middle, with the range), then **what actually matters**, then where on Hum it goes deeper. Anything marked **NEEDS SOURCE** is a figure Hum doesn't hold yet and would have to source (with a checked quote) before it could ship.

Calculated figures (Hum's own arithmetic on sourced numbers) are marked *(calc)*. Per-question figures use 0.34 Wh (0.24 to 1.86), the world-average grid 473 g CO2/kWh (125 to 560), and water as on-site cooling plus power-plant water.

## About you

### 1. Is using ChatGPT bad for the environment?
**Verdict: one question is tiny. The problem is the total, and where it's built.**
- One text question: about **0.34 Wh** (0.24 to 1.86). That's OpenAI's own figure; Google measured 0.24 Wh for Gemini; a large open model measured by MIT Technology Review used 1.86 Wh.
- CO2: about **0.16 g** per question *(calc)* (0.03 to 1.0 g).
- What matters: AI as a whole used roughly **155 TWh** in 2025 *(calc)* (128 to 184), and AI-focused data centres grew **50%** in 2025 (IEA). Your question is small; the build-out it's part of is not.
- Deeper: /question

### 2. How much electricity does one AI question use?
**Verdict: about a third of a watt-hour, but nobody outside the companies can check it.**
- 0.34 Wh (0.24 to 1.86). Low is Google's measured median, middle is OpenAI's stated average, high is a big open model measured independently.
- Why the range is wide: the figures count different things (chips only, or the whole data centre) and different models.
- NEEDS SOURCE: one everyday comparison (e.g. an LED bulb for a minute, a phone charge) so 0.34 Wh means something.
- Deeper: /question, How We Know

### 3. Does every AI question "drink" a bottle of water?
**Verdict: no. A text question is closer to a few drops to a teaspoon.**
- Per question: about **0.9 mL** *(calc)* (0.03 to 10 mL), counting cooling at the data centre plus water at the power plant.
- Most of it is at the power plant (2.18 L/kWh, US average), not the data centre (0.36 L/kWh, US average).
- What matters: water is local. A data centre in a dry place matters more than the world total.
- NEEDS SOURCE: the viral "bottle of water" claim itself, quoted from where it came from, so Hum can show why it differs.
- Deeper: /live (the pool)

### 4. Are AI images and videos worse than text?
**Verdict: yes, much. Video is the big one.**
- One image: about **1.22 Wh** (0.63 to 2.9), around 4 text questions *(calc)*.
- One short video clip: about **90 Wh** (25 to 944), around **265 text questions** *(calc)*.
- What matters: if you want to cut your own AI footprint, make fewer videos; text barely moves it.
- Deeper: /question (text, picture, video)

### 5. How big is my own AI footprint in a year?
**Verdict: for most people, small next to heating, driving or flying.**
- 100 text questions every day for a year: about **12 kWh** *(calc)* (9 to 68), about **6 kg CO2** *(calc)* on the world-average grid.
- NEEDS SOURCE: everyday yearly comparisons (a fridge's year, one flight, a km by car, a household's electricity) to put 12 kWh in context. This is the one answer that most needs them.
- This is where the "Your footprint" tool would live: pick your habits, get your year with the range.

### 6. Should I stop using AI to help the climate?
**Verdict: your own use isn't the lever. Where data centres get their power is.**
- The same question is about **8 times** dirtier on an India-like grid (708 g/kWh) than on London's grid on a clean day (82 g/kWh) *(calc)*.
- Company claims matter too (question 12).
- What you can do: fewer AI videos (question 4), ask the companies you use where their electricity comes from, hour by hour (question 12).
- NEEDS SOURCE: an honest "what moves your footprint most" list with sourced figures, so Hum doesn't moralise without numbers.
- Deeper: /2030 (choose the grids)

## About the world

### 7. How much electricity do data centres use?
**Verdict: as much as a big country, and more than France.**
- **485 TWh** in 2025 (IEA), about **1.5%** of the world's electricity (2024 figure).
- Already more than 8 of the 15 countries Hum compares: France (481 TWh), Mexico, Italy, the UK, Australia, Spain, Sweden and the Netherlands.
- CO2 from it: about **180 million tonnes** a year (2024, IEA).
- Deeper: home (the globe)

### 8. How much of that is AI?
**Verdict: about a quarter to well over a third, and rising fastest.**
- **26.5% to 38%** of data-centre electricity (a US figure from Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, used as a world estimate because no world figure is published).
- AI-focused data centres grew **50%** in 2025 (IEA); data centres overall have grown about **12%** a year since 2017.
- Deeper: home, How We Know

### 9. How fast is it growing? Where does it end up?
**Verdict: roughly double by 2030.**
- **485 TWh** in 2025 to **950 TWh** in 2030 (IEA). By 2035: about **1,200 TWh** in the IEA's base case (700 to 1,700).
- CO2: about **350 million tonnes** by 2035 (IEA), from 180 today.
- With high efficiency, the IEA still sees about **970 TWh** in 2035.
- Deeper: /2030

### 10. Is AI why my electricity bill is going up?
**Verdict: in some places it's part of it. Hum can't yet say how much for you.**
- In the eastern US, a **5,250 MW** jump in the PJM grid's demand forecast, almost all from data centres, helped drive its latest capacity auction results.
- In Ireland, data centres used **22%** of all metered electricity in 2024, up from 5% in 2015.
- NEEDS SOURCE: what that auction meant for household bills (the regulator's or a utility's own figure), and any other region's bill impact. This is one of the most-asked questions, so it's worth sourcing well.
- Deeper: "Claims to read twice" / impacts

### 11. Is there a big data centre near me?
**Verdict: probably near a big city. Hum tracks 27 hubs.**
- Biggest: **Northern Virginia**, about **3,000 MW** (2,550 to 11,300), about 2.4 times London's data centres (about 1,280 MW) *(calc)*.
- Others include Beijing (1,800 MW), Atlanta, Dallas-Fort Worth, Amsterdam, Singapore, Johor, Tokyo, Mumbai, Sydney.
- Local effects that are documented: gas turbines in Memphis, noise and land in Prince William County, Virginia.
- This becomes a "near you" lookup: pick your city, see the nearest hub, its size and its grid.

## About the claims

### 12. Aren't the tech companies running on 100% renewable energy?
**Verdict: on paper, mostly. On the actual grid, often not.**
- Many claims use renewable certificates bought anywhere, any time. Researchers estimate **42%** of committed electricity emission cuts won't result in real-world cuts if the trend continues.
- Matching a year's use with clean power has minimal long-run effect; matching it **hour by hour** keeps cutting emissions (peer-reviewed model).
- "Carbon neutral" through offsets: a review found **under 16%** of credits it studied were real reductions.
- Deeper: "Claims to read twice"

### 13. Won't more efficient chips fix this?
**Verdict: efficiency helps each question, but the total keeps rising.**
- US data centres went from **58 TWh** (2014) to **176 TWh** (2023) while getting more efficient. Researchers call this rebound.
- The IEA's high-efficiency case saves more than 15%, and still reaches about **970 TWh** by 2035.
- Deeper: "Claims to read twice"

### 14. Which AI uses the least energy?
**Verdict: nobody can tell you fairly yet.**
- Companies measure different things: Google's 0.24 Wh includes idle machines and the building; other figures count only the chips. Comparing them would be misleading.
- What Hum can show: each published figure with what it counts and who measured it (the v3 "why we can't tell you per model" explainer).

### 15. What else does AI cost besides electricity?
**Verdict: water, air, noise, land and chips, mostly felt locally.**
- Air: up to **247 MW** of gas turbines permitted at xAI's Memphis data centre.
- Land: a planned **2,139-acre**, 1.7 GW corridor near Manassas National Battlefield.
- Chips: generative AI could produce **1.2 to 5.0 million tons** of e-waste over 2020 to 2030 (peer-reviewed).
- Leaving out chips and buildings can miss **over 40%** of a data centre's emissions.
- Deeper: impacts on the home page

## What this draft shows

- **Hum already answers 11 of 15 well** from its own sources (1 to 4, 7 to 9, 11 to 15).
- **4 answers are thin without new sources:** everyday comparisons (2, 5), the "bottle of water" claim (3), "what moves your footprint" (6) and **electricity bills** (10). Comparisons and bills are what ordinary visitors care about most, so they're the first sourcing job.
- **Two tools fall out of it:** "Your footprint in a year" (5) and "Is there a data centre near me?" (11). Both give a visitor something about themselves, which is what keeps people past a minute.
- **The existing pages become the "go deeper" layer:** /question, /live, /2030, Daily and How We Know each sit behind two or three answers instead of being the front door.
