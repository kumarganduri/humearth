## Problem Statement

People hear "AI is bad for the planet" or "ChatGPT drinks a bottle of water" and have no way to tell what is true, how big it is, or what their own part is. The data exists, but it is scattered across IEA reports, company sustainability reports, research papers, and engineer-only calculators. Nothing makes it understandable for everyone, including a 10-year-old, in one beautiful place.

## What Makes This Cool

- **One unbroken camera move, from planet to person.** You land on a glowing 3D Earth where real AI data centers pulse with activity. Then the camera dives, with no cut, down to your own tiny world on the surface. That shot is the product.
- **Your world is alive.** You pick a biome: forest, coral reef, arctic, or jungle. Each of AI's three costs shows up as a different part of it:
  - CO2 shows as the trees and the sky: clear or hazy, leafy or bare.
  - Water shows as the river, lake, or reef level.
  - Energy shows as the light: warm daylight or fireflies versus a smoggy glow.
  - Animals appear when your world is healthy and hide (they never die) when it is stressed.
- **Hopeful balance.** Greener choices make your world bloom straight away: fewer image or video generations, smaller models, and in phase 2, prompting while the grid is clean or planting a real tree.
- **Honest by design.** Every number carries a low–high range and a "how do we know?" sources drawer.

## Constraints

- Solo builder, open source.
- Must work with no install, account, or backend, on a phone and in a classroom.
- Per-prompt numbers are estimates. Providers do not publish exact figures. Text prompts are tiny (about 0.24–0.34 Wh and 0.26–5 mL water); image and video generation are much larger.
- Privacy: nothing about a user's chats leaves their device.

## Premises (agreed)

1. The unique value is the **experience** (the globe, your world, being clear enough for a kid), not the measurement.
2. We reuse open-source estimation methods (EcoLogits per-model figures; AI Wattch / One Token Model style) and show sources and ranges for every number.
3. The extension only counts prompts and records the model and type. It never reads chat text, and it is open source.
4. The site works fully without the extension. A quick quiz gives you a world, so kids and phone users are not locked out.
5. Tone is hopeful balance: honest totals, no guilt over individual prompts. Text prompts barely move your world; image and video carry most of the visual weight.

## Landscape (2026 search)

- **Prompt-tracking extensions already exist:** AI Wattch (open source), ByteThirst (14 platforms), carbon-llm, AI Impact Tracker, GreenChat. All show numbers in a popup for adults.
- **Data-center maps already exist:** IEA Energy & AI Observatory, AIxEnergy (2,560 sites), Compute Atlas, DataCentersExposed, and an EU AI Data Center Map. All are aimed at analysts.
- **Gap:** nobody has built one grand, kid-clear, unified experience with a personal, living world. We build that layer and borrow the rest.

## Cross-Model Perspective (Claude subagent, cold read)

- **Coolest unconsidered version:** *grid-aware blooming*, using live carbon intensity from Electricity Maps or WattTime. The same prompt grows your world more when the grid is running on solar or wind. This gives "good actions" a real mechanic and teaches kids how the grid works. Also suggested: "Follow your prompt" (a particle travelling from home to a data center to a power plant and a named watershed), and a shared planetary canopy.
- **What excites the builder:** "you zoom down into your own personal forest on its surface." Protect that camera move above everything else.
- **What gets you 50% of the way:** AI Wattch for counting, EcoLogits for the figures, globe.gl / three-globe for the Earth, and IEA/AIxEnergy for site data. The other 50% (the world renderer, the seamless dive, the quiz mapping, honest comparison copy, and kid-level art) is the whole product.
- **Warning:** if a few text chats visibly wilt the world, the tone slides into guilt and misinforms kids. Calibrate the effects against real-world comparisons.

## Approaches Considered

- **A: Weekend Seed.** A static site with the globe, the dive into your world, a quiz, and share links. Chosen as phase 1.
- **B: Full Living Planet.** Everything in A, plus a counting-only extension (forked from or compatible with AI Wattch), grid-aware blooming, and a shared canopy on a small backend. Chosen as phase 2, built on top of A.
- **C: Follow-Your-Prompt story scroll.** Not chosen as the base because it reads like an article rather than a world you come back to. It is kept as a possible "how it works" section inside A/B.

## Recommended Approach: A, then B

### Phase 1: Seed (static, no backend). Weekend for the dive spike, ~2 weeks total

- **Stack:**
  - Vite + TypeScript + three.js, with **three-globe inside our own renderer**. We don't use globe.gl, because it owns the camera and the renderer, and the no-cut dive needs one shared scene.
  - The world is a procedural low-poly scene: instanced trees, a river and animals, generated from a seeded random generator.
  - Hosted as a static site on **Cloudflare Pages**, deployed automatically on push.
- **Build order (the risk goes first):**
  0. **Numbers first (OV #3, day 1):** constants.json (including video ranges), the k-derivation script, and the guardrail tests. If no curve passes across low/mid/high, settle the mapping before any UI.
  1. **The dive.** Spike it right after: one three.js scene, and a camera path from orbit down to a clearing. Handle the switch in detail level from globe to world by fading the globe out and fading the world tile in over the last ~10% of the path. If this doesn't feel magical, stop and rethink before building anything else.
  2. **AI-building hubs (OV #7):** 30–50 hub regions (for example Northern Virginia, Dublin, Singapore), each with a sourced regional capacity range. The JSON stores `{name, lat, lon, mw:{low,mid,high}, source, retrieved}`, and lantern size comes from mw.mid. This uses the same schema checks as the data pipeline.
  3. **Landing.** A night-side globe with pulsing sites, brightness proportional to est_MW. The "since you opened this page" ticker is built from **one named IEA figure** for global data-center electricity, multiplied by a stated AI share. Water is derived from that using a stated WUE factor. The ticker shows the result as bathtubs and homes, with a range and a sources drawer.
  4. **Quiz → numbers → world** (see the calibration below).
  5. **Greener-week toggles, the share link, and the fallbacks.**
- **Quiz (3 taps, no typing; OV #4):**
  | Question | Options | Maps to |
  |---|---|---|
  | AI questions per day | 0-2 / 3-9 / 10-49 / 50+ | 1 / 6 / 30 / 80 text prompts per day |
  | AI pictures | never / sometimes / a lot | 0 / 3 / 20 images per week |
  | AI videos | never / sometimes / a lot | 0 / 1 / 5 short clips per week |
  - The "Which AI" question is dropped. One blended per-prompt figure is used, with a range covering all major providers.
  - The biome question returns in Phase 1.5, when there is more than one biome.
- **Per-unit constants** (baked JSON, each stored as low / mid / high with a source URL):
  - **Energy and CO2:** EcoLogits per-model figures, plus Google's and OpenAI's published per-prompt disclosures as anchors. Grid intensity uses a global average.
  - **Water:** EcoLogits does not cover it fully. Water = energy × (on-site WUE plus off-site water per kWh from electricity generation). Both factors are named and sourced in the drawer.
- **Why the three channels differ:** each provider gets its own factors from its sustainability report: PUE, on-site WUE, and the grid carbon intensity where its data centers are. That way CO2, water, and energy don't simply move together. Where a provider publishes no data, the factor falls back to the global average, and the drawer says so. Channels will still be partly linked in Phase 1. Phase 2 grid-aware data separates them fully.
- **Calibration (derive before building the UI):**
  - For each channel c ∈ {CO2, water, energy}: health_c = 1 − clamp(weekly_c / (k × P_c), 0, 0.85). Here P_c is the per-person global AI average implied by the ticker baseline. The clamp floor is 0.15, so a world never goes fully dead.
  - **Guardrail tests** (these must pass, so the "no guilt" promise can be checked):
    - A "50+ text prompts/day, no images or video" user stays at ≥ 0.8 on every channel.
    - A "a lot of video" user drops below 0.5.
  - **Derive k offline first.** Write a script that runs both guardrails across the low, mid, and high constants and picks the multiplier k (starting guess: 4). If no single k passes both guardrails, change the curve (for example a log scale) rather than loosening the thresholds.
  - Animals appear when the average health is ≥ 0.7, some hide between 0.4 and 0.7, and most hide below 0.4.
  - Mapping to the world: CO2 → leaf density and haze; water → river level; energy → sun warmth versus smog glow.
- **Where the dive lands:** a spot chosen from the seed within a biome-appropriate band of latitude. It is **never the user's location**. The seed is random and created on the first visit.
- **Share link:** the URL hash holds the seed, the biome, and the **coarse quiz buckets** (not exact numbers). The link always shows your **real** world (decision 4A). If you turned on greener toggles, the hash also carries those toggles as "my plan". The friend sees a small badge (for example "My plan: fewer AI pictures") and a tappable "See what my world could be" preview, which is clearly labelled as a preview. There is no account and no personal data.
- **Fallbacks:**
  - When WebGL is unavailable: a static illustration of the world plus the numbers.
  - `prefers-reduced-motion`: the dive becomes a crossfade.
- **Targets:** 60 fps on a mid-range laptop and ≥ 30 fps on a recent phone. Layouts for each viewport are defined in decision 11A.
- **Skipped in Phase 1:** other biomes, the extension, accounts, a database, live grid data, and the shared canopy.

### Phase 1.5: More worlds

- Coral reef (water level = the health of the reef), arctic (the ice edge and penguins), and jungle, all using the same three channels and the same animal logic.

### Phase 2: Living Planet

- **Counting-only extension (Chrome/Firefox):**
  - Detects the platform, the model, and whether each request is text, image, or video, then counts it. It never reads chat content.
  - Daily bucketed totals update your world. The extension opens your world URL with a refreshed hash, so no server is needed.
- **Grid-aware blooming:** uses Electricity Maps / WattTime intensity for the likely data-center region.
- **Shared canopy:** everyone's worlds become tiles on the globe. This needs a small backend that stores aggregated counts only.
- **Optional real-world action:** a tree-planting partner (Ecologi / One Tree Planted).

Wireframe (rough flow): `~/.gstack/projects/ai-footprint/designs/mockup-20261001/sketch.png`. Its forest-only third screen will grow into the biome world described above.

## Experience Design (from /plan-design-review, 2026-10-01)

### Site map (decision 1A)
```
  [ Earth ] ──"Make my world"──> [ Quiz ] ──dive──> [ My World ]
      ^  \                                              |
      |   "Visit my world" (returning visitor, 1 tap) ───┘
      |                                                   |
      └────────────── "back to Earth" (reverse dive) ─────┘
  [ How We Know ]  reachable from the tiny nav + from any number
```
- **The site is 3 places:** Earth, My World, and How We Know.
- **Tiny persistent nav:** the wordmark at top-left, and "My World" and "How We Know" at top-right.
- **Returning visitor:** lands on Earth with *their* world glowing on the globe, plus one primary button, "Visit my world", which dives straight in. The seed and quiz buckets are saved in localStorage (a first visit uses the URL hash).

### Landing hierarchy (decision 2A)
1. **The globe** is the one visual anchor and fills the viewport.
2. **A one-line promise**, e.g. "Every AI question uses a little water and power. See yours."
3. **One primary button:** "Make my world" for a first visit, or "Visit my world" for a returning visitor.
4. **A small live ticker** in a solid text zone *below* the globe, never laid over the starfield.
- "How We Know" appears only in the nav and when you tap a number. There is no second button competing with the primary one.

### Interaction states (decision 3A)
| Place / feature | Loading | Empty | Error | Success | Partial |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Earth | A static globe image, the promise, and a primary button that already works. The 3D globe fades in when ready. No spinner. | n/a | Sites JSON fails: the globe still shows with no dots, a small note says "Couldn't load AI buildings", and the numbers still work. | The globe spins slowly and the dots pulse. | No WebGL: a static illustration plus the numbers. |
| Dive | n/a | n/a | Slow device (under 24 fps for 2 s): automatically switch to a crossfade and a lighter world. | Lands on My World. The nav is hidden during the dive and comes back on landing. | Skippable: tap or Esc jumps straight to the landed view. Reduced motion: crossfade. |
| My World | The world grows in from bare ground (it is also the reveal). | No quiz yet: a bare seedling plot, "Your world is waiting", and a "Make my world" button. | n/a | The world is alive (see the journey below). | n/a |
| Shared link | n/a | n/a | Bad or old hash: opens Earth with "That world moved away. Make your own?" | A friend's world: a banner reads "A friend's world" (no names are stored), with a "Make mine" button. | n/a |

### Share honesty (decision 4A)
- A shared world is always the real one. Greener choices travel as a labelled "my plan" badge plus an optional preview, never as the default view.

### Quiz flow (decision 5A)
- One question at a time in a bottom panel over the still-visible globe.
- Answers are big picture-buttons: tap only, with almost nothing to read.
- With each answer the globe turns a little toward "your spot". The last tap starts the dive.

### Landing in your world (decision 6A)
- For about 3 s after landing there is no UI. The world grows in, and animals appear or peek out.
- Then three small glyphs fade in: **leaf** (CO2), **drop** (water), and **sun** (energy). Tapping one shows its number, its range, and "what this means", with a link into How We Know.
- One friendly sentence names the biggest **activity** (OV #2), e.g. "Your river is a little low. AI pictures used most of it."
  - In Phase 1 the leaf, drop and sun honestly show one health value three ways. Phase 2 grid-aware data makes them diverge, and How We Know says so.
- There is no overall health bar or grade. (This replaces the bar in the wireframe.)

### Art direction: Tabletop diorama (decision 7A)
- **Earth:** a hand-painted toy globe floating in a soft dawn-blue sky gradient that comes from the scene's lighting, not black space and not neon.
- **Data centers:** tiny, warm-glowing blocks ("AI buildings") on the globe.
- **Worlds:** clay or papercraft low-poly dioramas on a round plot of land. Flat-shaded and saturated, with earthy colours.
- **Lighting:** one warm key light with soft, *offset* shadows. No glow halos or bloom.
- **Stress looks like weather, not death:** the sky gets hazy, leaves lose their colour, the river runs lower, and animals hide in burrows or trees. Blooming brings back colour, water, sunlight and animals.
- **Avoid:** near-black space with neon glow, purple gradients, glassmorphism, and stock-photo realism.
- **One consistent universe:** the globe and the worlds share materials, so the dive has no switch from realistic to cartoon.

### Copy glossary (decision 8A)
| Concept | Always say | Never say (on main screens) |
|---|---|---|
| Data center | AI buildings | data center, hyperscaler |
| Smaller or efficient model | a lighter AI | smaller model, parameters |
| Image / video generation | AI pictures / AI videos | generations, inference |
| First-visit CTA | Make my world | Grow my forest, Zoom in |
| Returning CTA | Visit my world | |
| CO2 / water / energy | air (leaf) / water (drop) / power (sun) | gCO2e, mL, Wh |
- Numbers are shown as things you can picture: bathtubs, minutes of running a fridge, balloons of CO2. Exact units and ranges appear only in How We Know.
- Sentences on the main screens are about 12 words or fewer.

### Greener choices live in the world (decision 9A)
- Greener choices are small objects at the edge of the diorama:
  - a **paintbrush** for "fewer AI pictures"
  - a **film reel** for "fewer AI videos"
  - a **feather** for "a lighter AI"
  - a **seed** for "plant a real tree" (phase 2 partner)
- Tapping one toggles it, and the world reacts straight away with bloom, water and light.
- "Share" and "Back to Earth" are two small icon buttons (with text labels for screen readers) in one corner.
- No cards or boxed panels on the world screen.

### Design system (decision 10A, DONE 2026-10-01)
- **Done:** see `~/.gstack/projects/ai-footprint/DESIGN.md` (name **Hum**, Grandstander / Atkinson Hyperlegible Next / Shantell Sans, toy-globe-on-a-windowsill palette, stress that goes quiet instead of red). Live preview: `designs/design-system-20261001/preview.html`.
- Every UI build task references DESIGN.md tokens. No hard-coded colours.

### Responsive and accessibility (decision 11A)
- **Phone (portrait):**
  - The globe fills the top ~65%. The promise and the button sit in a solid bottom zone.
  - The quiz is a bottom sheet with 2×2 picture buttons.
  - The world fills the screen, with the glyphs at the top and the choice objects along the bottom edge.
- **Desktop and tablet:** the same composition. On wide screens the text zone sits to the left of the globe.
- **Accessibility:**
  - Every action is a real button. Dragging the globe is optional and never required.
  - Keyboard order: promise, then primary button, then nav. Focus rings are visible and themed.
  - Touch targets are at least 44 px. Text contrast is at least 4.5:1, and body text is at least 16 px.
  - A live screen-reader sentence describes the world, e.g. "Your river is a little low; birds are hiding."
  - Reduced motion replaces the dive and the bloom with crossfades.
  - No information is carried by colour alone: glyphs have distinct shapes and word labels.
  - Landmarks: header (nav), main (the current place), and a region for How We Know.

### How We Know (decision 12A)
- **Tap any number:** a small panel opens right there, without leaving the world. It shows:
  - the range in kid words ("between 2 and 5 bathtubs")
  - a line on "why we're not totally sure"
  - a "See the full story" link
- **The How We Know page** is written for grown-ups and teachers: one reading column, 65–75 characters wide. It covers:
  - the methods and every source, with links
  - the assumptions (the AI share, WUE, and grid factors)
  - a dated "we changed our numbers" log
- The "sources drawer" mentioned elsewhere in this doc means this tap panel.

### Sound (decision 13A)
- Sound is muted at the start. A small speaker icon in the nav reads "Sound on".
- When sound is on:
  - soft wind during the dive
  - gentle chimes and birdsong when the world blooms
  - a quiet river loop in your world
- The choice is remembered locally. Sound is never required to understand anything.

- **Localisation and RTL:** later. The glossary makes translation easier.

## Engineering Review (/plan-eng-review, 2026-10-02)

- **Scope (D1):** full Phase 1 as planned. There is no separate M0 gate; the dive spike still goes first in the build order.

- **Dive rendering (1A):** two scenes and one renderer, with a camera handoff.
  ```
  t: 0 ──────────────── 0.85 ──── 0.95 ──── 1.0
  globeCam: orbit → descend to seed spot ──┐
                                           ├─ crossfade (both rendered to targets, blended)
  worldCam:                 high above plot ┴→ settle at clearing
  ```
  - The globe scene and the world scene each have their own near/far planes, so there is no depth flicker.
  - three-globe `setPointOfView()` is called every frame while the globe camera moves.
  - After the handoff the globe scene stops rendering, so the world gets the whole GPU budget.
  - The reverse dive runs the same timeline backwards.
  - Skipping jumps to t = 1. Reduced motion jumps to a 350 ms crossfade.

- **App state and ownership (2A):** one explicit state machine, written as pure functions and unit-tested.
  ```
  boot ─┬─ hash valid & seed≠mine ──> earth(friendWorld) ──dive──> world(owner=friend)
        ├─ hash valid & seed=mine ──> earth(myWorld)
        ├─ hash invalid/old ────────> earth("That world moved away")
        ├─ localStorage world ──────> earth(myWorld, "Visit my world")
        └─ nothing ─────────────────> earth("Make my world")
  earth ─Make my world─> quiz(1..5) ─last tap─> diving ─> world(owner=me) ─back─> reverseDiving ─> earth
  world(owner=friend) ─"Make mine"─> quiz(1)
  world(owner=me) ─"Change my answers"─> quiz(prefilled) ─save─> world(owner=me, same seed)
  ```
  - **Rule:** localStorage holds *my* world. A URL hash is a view-only friend world, and it is never written to storage.
  - Corrupt or blocked storage (private mode) is treated as "nothing". There is no crash.

- **Share link codec (3A):** the hash is `#w1.` followed by base64url of a fixed binary layout:
  - `seed:uint32`
  - `biome:u8 enum`
  - `buckets:[q1 0–3, pics 0–2, vids 0–2]` (OV #4)
  - `plan:u8 bitmask`

  The decoder accepts only those numbers and enums, so no raw text ever reaches the DOM. An unknown version or any out-of-range field gives the "moved away" state. Worlds recompute from buckets using the *current* constants. Tests: round-trip, fuzz on random strings, and every out-of-range field.

- **Data pipeline (4A):**
  ```
  data/sources/ (EcoLogits models.json [MPL-2.0 header kept], provider reports, IEA figure, sites CSV)
     └─> scripts/build-data.ts ─validate─> public/data/constants.json + sites.json
                                   │        (constantsVersion, contentHash)
                                   └─diff vs previous─> append to the How We Know change log
  ```
  - CI fails the build if any value is missing low ≤ mid ≤ high, a unit, a source URL, or a retrieved date.

- **Footprint engine (5A):** `src/footprint/` is pure TypeScript with no three.js or DOM imports.
  ```
  buckets + plan + constants ─> weeklyUse{co2,water,energy}:{low,mid,high}
                             ─> health{co2,water,energy} ∈ [0.15,1]
                             ─> animals: present|some-hiding|hiding
                             ─> sentenceKey + kidComparison (glass/bathtub/fridge-minutes)
  ```
  - The quiz result, glyphs, sentence, tap panel, ticker, How We Know table and 3D world all read from this one output.

- **Design tokens (6A):** `scripts/build-tokens.ts` reads the DESIGN.md front matter and generates `src/tokens.ts` (typed) and `src/tokens.css` (CSS variables).
  - The 3D code reads colours only through `sceneColor(token)`, which does the sRGB→linear conversion once.
  - The renderer sets `outputColorSpace = SRGBColorSpace` in a single place.
  - CI fails if the generated files are stale.

- **WebGL context loss (7A):**
  - On `webglcontextlost` (with preventDefault), show the static poster or illustration plus the numbers straight away.
  - On `webglcontextrestored`, rebuild the scenes from the current app state and fade back in.
  - Pause the render loop while the tab is hidden.
  - Tested by forcing loss through `WEBGL_lose_context`.

- **Test strategy (8A):** all 33 paths from the coverage map are covered.
  - **Vitest unit tests:**

- **Guardrail rule (decided 2026-10-02 during E2, after running the real numbers):** the world is drawn from the mid figures.
  - The guardrails are strict at the mid and high figures: a 50+/day text-only user stays ≥ 0.8, and a video-heavy user stays < 0.5.
  - At the low figures only the order is kept (a video-heavy week is never healthier than a text-heavy one). Holding all three levels strictly is impossible: mid text-heavy (190 Wh/wk) uses more than low video-heavy (163 Wh/wk), because the low video figure is an older, tiny model.
  - The baseline is all data-centre electricity per person, about 975 Wh/week (IEA 415 TWh, UN 8.16 bn), not an AI share, so every term is sourced.
  - Result: k = 9.56. The workable range is narrow, [9.40, 9.72), so any data change re-runs `derive:k`, and the build fails if the guardrails break.
  - The "Which AI" figures are not vendored from EcoLogits. The blended quiz (OV #4) uses published per-prompt disclosures instead, so the MPL file is no longer needed.
